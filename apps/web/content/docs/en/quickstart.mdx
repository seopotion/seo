This is the shortest path from a new account to an article on your blog. It takes about fifteen
minutes of your attention, spread across a couple of waits.

Read the [free vs. paid](#4-where-free-and-paid-diverge) section before you start. The automatic
content plan — the part that makes Seopotion feel automatic — is a paid feature, and knowing that
up front saves you looking for a button that is not there.

## 1. Sign in

Seopotion uses **Google sign-in only**. Select **Join with Google** and pick the Google account you
want to own the workspace. There is no email-and-password option and no separate sign-up step: the
first sign-in creates your account.

You may occasionally be asked to sign in again. Signing back in returns you exactly where you
were.

## 2. Scan your website

The first onboarding step is **Website Scan**. Enter your business website URL and select **Scan
website**.

The scan reads your site and pre-fills the rest of onboarding — your business description, your
audience, your blog details — so you are correcting a draft instead of filling in a blank form.
Leave the page open while it runs; you will see *Scanning website content. This can take a moment.*
and then *Scan complete. Generated setup details have been added to the next steps.*

Everything the scan produces is editable, both now and later from Settings. If something is wrong,
fix it here — the software builds your content plan from these answers.

## 3. Review your business profile

The scan drops you into five review steps. None of them is long, and all of them are editable
afterwards.

1. **About Your Business** — business name, content language, country, and a business description.
   Language and country matter more than they look: they set the language your articles are
   written in and the search market they target.
2. **Target Audience And Competitors** — who you are writing for, and competitor website URLs.
   Competitors feed keyword research directly.
3. **Blog Info** — your sitemap URL, your blog page URL, and up to three of your best existing
   posts.
4. **Article Settings** — article style, how many internal links to include, whether to add a table
   of contents, and whether to embed YouTube videos.
5. **Integrations** — which publishing integration you plan to use, and how you heard about us.
   You can skip connecting WordPress here and do it later from Settings.

Select **Get Started** on the last step. You will see *You're ready to start.*

## 4. Where free and paid diverge

This is the fork.

**On a paid plan (Growth or Business)**, finishing setup immediately queues a **30-day
content plan**. Keyword research runs against your business profile and competitors, and the
highest-opportunity keywords are spread across the next 30 days — one article per day. Open
**Content planner** and you will see *Your 30-day content plan is in progress*; the page says to
expect this to take a few minutes and refreshes itself when the plan lands.

**On the free plan**, no content plan is created. You get the full workspace, the calendar, and
your business profile, but:

- **Keyword research** is unavailable — the **Research Keywords** button is marked *Paid feature*.
- **Keyword scheduling** is unavailable — the **+ Choose keyword** button on each empty calendar
  date is disabled, and opening it shows *Keyword scheduling is a paid feature*.
- Because nothing gets scheduled, nothing gets generated. The calendar stays empty.

To move forward, open **Billing** and pick a plan:

| Plan | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| Free | Workspace, business profile, article settings, WordPress connection, Search Console |
| **Growth** | Everything above, plus keyword research, keyword scheduling, and your content calendar |
| **Business** | Everything in Growth, plus human review of up to 10 selected Seopotion-generated articles per month |

Both paid tiers unlock the same software features. Business adds a human quality-control layer for
up to 10 selected Seopotion-generated articles per month. Review may cover brand and business fit,
readability, structure, internal-link relevance, on-page SEO fundamentals, factual red flags, and
reasonable edits. It does not include agency campaign management, human-written-from-scratch
content, backlink outreach, paid advertising, or external marketing distribution.

## 5. Watch the first articles arrive

Articles are generated on the date they are scheduled for, not all at once — that is what the
calendar is for. So the plan appears in full immediately, and the articles fill in day by day.

In **Content planner**, each calendar day shows its keyword with volume and difficulty, and a badge
that tracks it: **Planned** while it waits, then **Generated** once the article exists. Selecting a
day opens the article editor, where you can revise the title, slug, body, meta title, meta
description and featured image before it goes anywhere.

If a day's generation fails, its keyword is marked **Failed** in the Target Keywords table. Nothing
is lost — the keyword stays in your pool and can be scheduled onto another date.

You do not have to wait for the plan to finish before doing anything else. Steps 2, 3 and 6 are all
useful while it runs.

## 6. Connect WordPress and publish

Generated articles stay in Seopotion until you publish them manually or enable automatic
publishing for future articles.

1. Follow [Publish to WordPress](/docs/wordpress) to install the plugin and connect it. It takes
   about five minutes and you only do it once.
2. Open **Content history** and select an article.
3. Select **Send to WordPress**.

Whether the post arrives as a draft or goes live is decided by the **Post Mode** setting inside the
WordPress plugin, so you stay in control of what appears on your site.

If you want a hands-off workflow, open **Article settings** and enable **Auto-publish**. This
setting applies to future generated articles and can be turned off whenever you want to return to
manual review.
